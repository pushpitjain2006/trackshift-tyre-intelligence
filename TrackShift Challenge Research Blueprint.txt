	8. Problem 1 — Energy & Overtake Intelligence
OFFICIAL: "Optimizing finite electrical energy deployment while balancing immediate overtake opportunities against long-term race performance and rule compliance"1.
RESEARCH-BACKED: The 2026 Formula 1 technical regulations dictate a massive paradigm shift to a roughly 50/50 power distribution. The Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) drops to approximately 400kW, while the MGU-K (Kinetic Motor Generator Unit) output increases massively to 350kW—a nearly 300% increase in electrical reliance9. Recuperable energy is capped at 8.5 MJ per lap. Furthermore, a novel "Manual Override Mode" has been introduced, allowing up to 350kW deployment up to 337kph for a following car, providing +0.5MJ of extra energy specifically to facilitate overtaking9.
DEDUCTION: This is fundamentally a Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) and Model Predictive Control (MPC) problem11. State variables include the kinetic energy of the vehicle and the Battery State of Charge (SoC), while the constraints are hard regulatory limits on MJ deployment per lap12. Real-time optimal control requires resolving "bang-bang" control strategies (full deployment vs. full regeneration) under stringent boundary conditions, while simultaneously managing multi-agent game theory regarding defending versus attacking behaviors12.
The primary constraint for a hackathon team is data availability. Telemetry for battery SoC and specific MGU-K deployment modes is highly guarded proprietary data. Public APIs provide speed, throttle, and RPM, but inferring exact hybrid deployment modes from public data introduces massive uncertainty15.
9. Problem 2 — Track Limits Detection
OFFICIAL: "Automating line-crossing detection from race video footage to streamline steward decisions without losing human accuracy"1.
RESEARCH-BACKED: In modern Formula 1, track limits are strictly defined by the solid white boundary line. A violation occurs only if all four wheels of the vehicle completely cross the outer edge of this white line17. The FIA is actively investigating "Computer Vision" methodologies—specifically shape analysis and pixel counting—to replace the manual, human-intensive review processes currently employed17.
DEDUCTION: The technical requirement involves a multi-stage computer vision pipeline. The system must perform vehicle detection, instance segmentation of the four individual wheels to establish the contact patches, line detection for the track boundary, and crucially, a homography transformation (perspective geometry) to map the 2D broadcast camera plane into a top-down, bird's-eye view17. This is necessary to mathematically ascertain if the wheel contact patch pixels exceed the track line pixels.
The complexity here is exceedingly high. The system must achieve sub-pixel accuracy while handling occlusion, motion blur at speeds exceeding 300 km/h, and varying lighting conditions. Furthermore, public datasets of high-framerate, static-camera F1 track limit violations are practically non-existent. Synthesizing or manually annotating sufficient data to train a robust segmentation model during a 24-hour hackathon carries extreme execution risk.
10. Problem 3 — Tyre Degradation Intelligence
OFFICIAL: "Isolating true tyre wear rates from confounding practice variables like fuel weight, traffic, and track evolution"1.
RESEARCH-BACKED: In motorsport physics, lap times naturally increase as tyres degrade and lose mechanical grip. However, this degradation curve is masked by fuel burn. In Formula 1, cars start the race with a maximum of 110 kg of fuel, which burns off linearly throughout the stints, making the car lighter and consequently faster21. Additional variables such as track evolution (the circuit "rubbering in" and gaining grip) and traffic (running in turbulent "dirty air") further confound the raw lap time observations22.
DEDUCTION: This is a classic hidden state estimation problem. A Bayesian state-space model or an advanced Kalman filter represents the mathematically optimal approach. The observed lap time can be modeled as a function of the latent, unobservable tyre state, the deterministically estimated fuel mass, and stochastic noise21.
The data availability for this specific problem is exceptional. The open-source fastf1 Python API provides highly accurate, sub-second telemetry, compound data, stint lengths, and environmental weather data (track temperature, air temperature)15. Fuel weight, while not explicitly broadcast, can be deterministically estimated assuming a linear burn rate from 110kg down to near zero by race end21.

14. Motorsport Engineering Principles
The core TrackShift philosophy demands abstracting the highly specific motorsport problem into a generalized engineering principle1.
The Energy & Overtake problem abstracts into the principle of dynamic constrained optimization of finite resources under multi-agent adversarial conditions. In the real world, this translates to systems that must allocate a strictly limited resource while reacting to the unpredictable actions of competitors or environmental variables.
The Track Limits problem abstracts into the principle of real-time visual compliance and edge-case anomaly detection utilizing spatial transformations. This is applicable wherever physical boundaries must be strictly monitored without human intervention, requiring 2D to 3D spatial mapping.
The Tyre Degradation problem abstracts into the principle of isolating hidden system state degradation from multi-variate, noisy, and confounded continuous observations. This principle governs any complex physical system where the true health of the core component cannot be directly measured, but must be inferred from the overall performance output which is clouded by external variables.

Project Title: State-Space Degradation Intelligence: From Paddock to Payload
Track Selection: Tyre Degradation Intelligence
Team Skills Profile: Data Science, Full-Stack Engineering, Operations Research
Problem Understanding: In Formula 1, raw lap times are a highly deceptive metric for analyzing tyre wear. Because continuous fuel burn (up to 110kg per race) makes the vehicle progressively lighter and faster, the true performance degradation of the rubber is mathematically masked. Similarly, in India's rapidly expanding commercial EV sector, apparent battery range and performance are heavily confounded by fluctuating cargo payloads and extreme ambient temperatures.
Proposed Solution & Technical Approach: Our team will build a Bayesian state-space filter utilizing data ingested from the FastF1 API. By treating tyre wear as a hidden Markov state, and treating fuel mass and track temperature as observable confounders, our algorithm mathematically isolates the true degradation curve. We will utilize PyMC for probabilistic inference, specifically applying a skewed-t distribution to account for asymmetric driver errors (e.g., lock-ups cost time, but they never save time).
Translation & Real-World Impact: We will directly port this mathematical framework to Indian EV fleets. By stripping away the noise of payload and traffic, fleet operators can isolate the true chemical degradation (State of Health) of their batteries. This predictive capability prevents premature battery replacements and stranded vehicles on logistics routes, saving an estimated 10-15% in annualized battery CapEx for massive logistics networks like Delhivery or BluSmart.
